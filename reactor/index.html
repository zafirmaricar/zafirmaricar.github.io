      How it works

      The plant

      The plant is a small modular reactor, sized from NuScale's published module: 250 MW of heat, 77 MW of electricity, water at 138 bar so it can't boil. Heat goes from the fuel to the primary water, from that to the steam generator, and the steam spins the turbine. Unlike older plants it can cool itself for days with no power at all: steam condenses in a heat exchanger in the pool and runs back by gravity.

      Neutrons

      Power follows point kinetics with six groups of delayed neutrons for uranium-235. The delayed ones, under 1% of the total, arrive seconds to a minute late, and they are what make a reactor controllable by hand:

      dn/dt = (ρ − β) / Λ · n + Σ λiCi + S
      dCi/dt = βi / Λ · n − λiCi

      Here n is power relative to full, ρ the reactivity, β = 0.0065 the delayed fraction, Λ = 20 µs the prompt generation time, Ci the precursor groups and S a weak source so a shut-down core still shows counts. Each step is solved implicitly in closed form, and split finer when the core goes prompt critical.

      Feedback

      Reactivity comes from the rods (an S-shaped worth curve), boron in the water (−8 pcm per ppm), fuel temperature (Doppler, −3 pcm/°C), water temperature (−20 pcm/°C) and steam voids. Both temperature effects are negative, so pulling rods raises power only until the hotter fuel and water cancel what you added. The sandbox shows that taking the protection away doesn't make the reactor explode; it takes more than one mistake to damage the core.

      Heat and water

      The fuel and core water are lumped nodes. The loop is a chain of cells that carry water round at the flow rate, and the same cells colour the pipes you see. When the pump stops, hot water rising from the core and cold water sinking from the steam generator keep it turning at a few percent: natural circulation. After a trip, decay heat keeps coming, about 6% at first, 1.5% after an hour, fitted as six decaying groups.

      What's simplified

      One loop instead of several, lumped fuel, a simple pressurizer and steam generator, no xenon yet, and setpoints that are illustrative rather than any real plant's. It runs at a fixed 60 steps a second, switching to longer steps once the reactor is shut down so a week of passive cooling plays in seconds.

      Written from scratch in JavaScript and Canvas 2D, no libraries. Source on GitHub.
